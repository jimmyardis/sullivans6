The Archive

A Memory Palace

135 years of accumulation. Drag to excavate.

May 1891 Lot Purchased

Diocesan trustees acquire the Sullivan's Island lot using funds linked to the dissolved Grace Church — a congregation that had held services in a borrowed Presbyterian building for nearly six years.

Aug. 1, 1891 The Contract

Robert McCarrel signs the construction contract. Rubble granite. Medieval Gothic. Builder, Charleston SC.

Sept. 12, 1891 Cornerstone

The cornerstone is laid in a named ceremony. Design: W. W. Deveaux, New York. Style: Medieval Gothic. Material: rubble granite.

July 10, 1892 First Service

The first Holy Communion is held. Officiant: Rev. George F. Degan. Cost of the completed chapel: slightly over $6,000.

Sept. 12, 1895 Consecration

Bishop Ellison Capers consecrates the Chapel of the Holy Cross on its fourth birthday. A stained-glass window is installed in memory of Miss Ella M. Benjamin. A silver communion service is dedicated to the Faithful Departed.

$6,000 The Build Cost

The total reported cost of constructing the Chapel of the Holy Cross — a deep revelation of material economy, given the structural weight of the rubble granite walls.

1902 Condemnation

The federal government initiates condemnation proceedings as Fort Moultrie expands its Endicott-era coastal defense network: disappearing carriages, minefields, rapid-fire guns aimed at the harbor mouth.

$9,000 The Acquisition Price

The federal government's compensation to the Episcopal diocesan trustees. The strongest documentary evidence converges on $9,000 — though the precise year of final transfer (1904 vs. 1905) remains contested.

1907 Keys Surrendered

The congregation strips the memorial stained glass and silver communion service. Keys are formally transferred. The Chapel of the Holy Cross becomes a Fort Moultrie post chapel.

June 1908 New Chapel Opens

First services held at the new Chapel of the Holy Cross, 2520 Middle Street. The memorial windows, beams, and chancel have been relocated. The congregation follows its artifacts.

1952 Lutheran Mission

Pastor J. Marion Rhoden is appointed. Fifty charter members begin worshipping in "the old Fort Moultrie Chapel." The same granite walls. Different hymns.

1972 Deconsecration

The Lutheran congregation relocates to Isle of Palms, becoming St. Mark's. For the first time in eighty years, the building serves no congregation. The chapel is deconsecrated and converted to private residential use.

$640,000 2002 Purchase

The current owner acquires 1401 Middle Street. A plan to relocate the entire structure to the I'On community in Mount Pleasant is subsequently proposed — and blocked by local preservation action.

2009–2010 The Keep

New World Byzantine Architecture completes the Romanesque keep addition. Reinforced concrete, massive cypress timbers, geothermal heat pump. Mandate: design something that looked older than the Gothic church it was joining.

W. G. Mazyck The Lay Reader

Lay reader and treasurer. Held Episcopal services in a borrowed Presbyterian church on Sullivan's Island for nearly six years before the Chapel of the Holy Cross was completed. Called one of the "founders of the mission" by the 1957 diocesan history.

The Sword Legend Mounting

A sword mounting in the castle interior. Its provenance unwritten — its weight felt in every room.
